## Claude

You're probably not doing anything wrong so much as doing the visible 10% of what they do. "Post" is the part they can explain in one word, but it's rarely the whole story.

**Posting a poster is passive, and passive doesn't convert.** A WhatsApp status or Facebook post gets seen by people who mostly aren't shopping for a contract or fibre right now. Even when someone is interested, they won't text a stranger-ish poster. Most high performers I'd bet on are doing direct, personal outreach and calling it "posting."

**What they're likely actually doing:**

1. **Messaging people directly.** Not broadcasting, but sending individual messages: "Hey, quick one, is your contract still with X? I can check if you'd get a better deal." Personal and low-pressure beats a poster every time.
2. **Asking every customer for referrals.** After every sale (or even a "no"), they ask: "Who else do you know who needs fibre/an upgrade/data?" One sale becomes two or three leads.
3. **Working specific groups.** Community Facebook groups, estate/complex groups, workplace groups, church or gym circles, WhatsApp communities. Not just their own contacts.
4. **Targeting people with a trigger.** Someone whose contract is ending, someone with a cracked phone, someone complaining about their wifi or load shedding, someone who just moved. Timing matters more than reach.
5. **Volume.** 5-12 sales a day usually means 40-100 conversations a day. If your closing rate is the same as theirs but you're talking to 10 people, you'll land at 1-2 sales.

**Things you can do:**

- **Ask your top colleague to show you, not tell you.** "Can I sit with you for an hour and watch how you work your referrals?" Most people say yes, and you'll learn more than from any tip list.
- **Track your numbers for a week.** How many people did you message, how many replied, how many became sales? That shows whether your problem is reach, replies, or closing.
- **Start with 20 personal messages a day** to people you actually know or have some link to, with a specific offer rather than a poster.
- **Ask your first few buyers for two names each.**

You're not bad at this. You've just been using the low-effort version of the method. Commission sales rewards outbound conversation, and that's a skill you can learn quickly.

## Claude

Your message isn't bad, but it's weak in a few specific ways, and that's probably why nothing comes back.

**What's going wrong**

1. **You're asking for a favour at the moment the customer feels nothing.** "Your data is activated" is a boring moment. They got what they wanted and are done thinking about it. The ask lands as an afterthought.
2. **Two asks in one.** "Post this" and "shoot them my number" compete. People do neither. The post ask is the worst one: nobody wants to put a stranger's poster on their status.
3. **"If you know anyone" is too vague.** The brain draws a blank. Nobody sits there scanning their contact list for people who need airtime or data. It has to be specific.
4. **There's no reason for them to bother.** Nothing in it for them, and nothing makes it easy.
5. **It reads like a template.** If everyone gets the same blast, people ignore it like spam.

**What works better**

Make it one ask, specific, easy, and with a reason. Ideas:

- **Ask for a name, not a post.** "Who's one person in your family or at work who pays too much for data? Send me their number and I'll sort them out the same way."
- **Offer something in return**, if your company allows it. Even a small thing: "If they sign up, I'll sort you out with a bonus/freebie" (only if you can actually deliver it).
- **Time it right.** Send the ask a day or two later, after they've actually used the data and are happy. "Hey, is the data working well? 🙌" Wait for the reply, then ask. A person who just said "yes, it's great" is far more likely to help.
- **Make it effortless.** Give them a one-line message they can forward: "Hey, this guy got me sorted with data, here's his number."

**Example flow**

Day 0: "Your data is active! Let me know if anything isn't working."
Day 1-2: "Hey, how's the data treating you?"
After they reply positively: "Glad to hear it! Quick one: who do you know that's always complaining about data or airtime? Send me their name and number and I'll look after them just like I did you."

That is a small, specific, easy ask at a moment when they like you.

**One more thing.** Even with a perfect ask, only maybe 1 in 5 or 10 customers will give a referral. That's normal. So your referral numbers depend on how many customers you ask, which circles back to volume. Ask everyone, every time, and track how many say yes.
